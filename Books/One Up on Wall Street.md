This book addresses the psychology of [[Value Investing]] and provides all the basics required to make good investments. 

If a stock declines should you ...
- sell before it goes down more
- hold because stocks almost always recover
- buy more its on sale

As long as your thesis is still intact, you should buy more. 

If you can't stomach the market disagreeing with you, investing in extremely boring industries that no one talks about, or better yet hates, or doubling down on a stock that you've already lost 30% on, [[Value Investing]] might not be for you. 

[[Peter Lynch]] lays out 4 types of companies
### 1. **[[Slow Growers]]**
**Definition**: Large, mature companies growing earnings at 2-5% per year. 

**Characteristics**: High dividends, stable industries, steady earnings.

**Investment**: Good for conservative investors seeking income.

### 2. **[[Stalwarts]]**
**Definition**: Established companies growing at 10-12% per year. 

**Characteristics**: Well-known names, stable but not explosive growth.

**Investment**: Balance between growth and stability, medium to long-term hold.

### 3. **[[Fast Growers]]**
**Definition**: Companies with earnings growth of 20-25% or more per year. 

**Characteristics**: Smaller, high potential, volatile. 

**Investment**: Higher risk, long-term horizon, look for competitive advantages.

### 4. **[[Cyclicals]]**
**Definition**: Companies that rise and fall with the economy. 

**Characteristics**: Fluctuating earnings, industries like autos and steel. 

**Investment**: Buy in downturns, sell in upswings, understand economic trends.
