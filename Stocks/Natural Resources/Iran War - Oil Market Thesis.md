## Thesis

The Iran War will last significantly longer than consensus expects. Brent crude likely reaches $150–200 within 90–180 days. Trading Brent rather than WTI — Trump export restrictions could suppress WTI relative to global prices.

**Preferred structure:** $120/$200 call spread on Jan 2027 Brent (expires Nov 25, 2026), sized at 5% of portfolio max loss. No Polymarket hedge (see revised assessment below). Duration stays sub-6 months — captures the spike phase before recession dynamics become relevant in 2027.

**The core mispricing:** The market is simultaneously (1) in backwardation — paying a scarcity premium today while pricing in eventual resolution — and (2) still under-pricing the current shock per Johnston's "show-me path." Prices grind higher as visible inventory drawdowns accumulate, not on speculation. Front month at ~$106 is suppressed by Trump noise and inventory buffers, not by physical reality.

**Peace deal ≠ price collapse.** Even a formal deal only drops prices $10–20 initially (speculative positioning unwinding). Physical supply takes months to restore regardless — producers won't restart wells until shipping is confirmed safe, then a 3-month ramp to ~90–95%. A peace deal announcement is a buying opportunity on the dip, not a thesis killer.

### The pipeline delay — why prices haven't fully moved yet

This is the mechanism behind Johnston's "show-me path." The Strait closed Feb 28, but ships already in transit kept arriving for weeks:

- **Asia/Europe:** last ships arrived March–April
- **US West Coast:** longest transit route (~35–45 days) — last ship arrived ~May 14 **(now)**
- Refinery processing lag adds another 2–3 weeks before product shortfalls become visible at the pump

**The implication:** the consumer-visible shortage doesn't begin until June 2026. EIA weekly inventory data over the next 4–6 weeks is the "show-me" catalyst the market has been waiting for. Art Berman (oil analyst): *"even if this war were to end today, the inherent lags in our industrial supply chains mean shortfalls are already baked into the coming months."*

IEA May 2026 report: projects global inventory drawdown of **8.5 mb/d in Q2 2026**, with steepest draws in May–June. At that rate, excess commercial inventory buffers are gone in roughly 6–8 weeks.

## Supply Dynamics

- **~14 mb/d shut in** (IEA May 2026); EIA reported 10.5 mb/d average in April, peaking ~10.8 mb/d in May as storage limits force additional shut-ins. Historically unprecedented — 1973 embargo was ~4–5 mb/d and caused a 400% price spike
- Strait of Hormuz carries ~17–21 mb/d; effective closure cuts off Saudi, UAE, Kuwait, Iraq exports
- **OPEC+ spare capacity largely irrelevant** — Strait closure means they can't get barrels to market. Saudi East-West pipeline (Yanbu bypass) has limited capacity, already near full
- **SPR inadequate at scale, and getting more so:**
	- Inventory: 316.5 million barrels as of July 10, 2026 (lowest since April 1983), down from ~400mb when this note was started in May. ~98.9mb drawn since the war started (Feb 28) as part of the 172mb IEA-coordinated release.
	- Draw pace so far (~0.65–0.7 mb/d average) is well within capacity — no rate problem yet. But the ceiling is degraded: GAO-26-106918 (May 2026) put effective drawdown capability at 61% of design (2.7 of 4.415 mb/d), with Big Hill fully offline (construction outage) and West Hackberry at 58%. Against a ~13–14 mb/d global deficit, even full-rate SPR delivery couldn't offset a third of the gap — capacity, not barrels remaining, is the real constraint.
	- **No legal floor stops further release.** The often-cited "252.4mb minimum" (EPCA §6241(h)) only restricts a minor, unused drawdown authority (capped at 30mb/event, 60 days). The authority actually in use — §6241(d), a unilateral presidential "severe energy supply interruption" finding, no congressional vote required — has no barrel floor and no duration cap. Same mechanism behind 1991, 2005, 2011, 2022, and this release.
	- **Not a wartime-demand story.** DOD's own consumption (~270–360k b/d, <2% of US total) is too small to matter — the SPR draw here is backfilling lost Strait-of-Hormuz *supply*, not meeting extra *war-fighting* demand. Consistent with this thesis's supply-side framing throughout.
- **China/Japan strategic reserves:** heavily oil-dependent, long-term thinkers — won't aggressively draw down without clear path to resolution. Maybe 2–3 mb/d combined max draw, still net deeply negative
- **~1 billion barrels unproduced by summer 2026**
- **Restart sequence after resolution:** (1) peace deal → (2) insurance market normalization (weeks) → (3) shipping companies return (weeks–months) → (4) producers restart wells (only once shipping confirmed) → (5) 3-month ramp to ~90–95% → never fully 100%. Total: 4–6 months minimum from deal to meaningful supply restoration
