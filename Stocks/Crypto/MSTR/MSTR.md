## Overview
MSTR is a bitcoin treasury company. They own bitcoin on leverage.

MSTR Capital Stack:
- Convertible Debt — most has 0-2% interest rate, conversion accounted for in model
- Preferred Shares — STRF, [[STRC]], STRK, STRD
- Common Equity

I made a spreadsheet that calculates MSTR's nav under two scenarios: (1) none of the convertible debt converts, (2) all convertible debt converts at current prices.

## Thesis
MSTR should trade around 1 mnav (multiple to nav).

### Arguments for above 1 mnav
- Bitcoin exposure — allows some institutions restricted from owning bitcoin directly or through ETFs to gain exposure. Premium justified by this should significantly come down with increased market cap.
- Bitcoin Earnings — new accounting rules allow gains on Bitcoin valuation to be counted as earnings. This is a stupid argument, be wary of anyone who suggests this.
- Intelligent Leverage — MSTR's capital structure is designed to weather Bitcoin downturns and has a time horizon of 4+ years (length of previous Bitcoin cycles). Convertible debt is quite ingenious because they can sell their own vol, which is higher than BTC vol because of higher than 1 mnav. Preferred shares yielding 8-13% seem pretty unintelligent — steep price to pay for flexibility. Ultimately the only reason their leverage is better than what an individual could do is because of the higher than 1 mnav. Alone doesn't justify above 1 mnav.
- BTC Yield — MSTR has had a great track record of increasing Bitcoin per share: ~70% in 2024 and ~25% YTD. Only reason for this is that they have higher than 1 mnav and can do accretive dilution. Best argument imo. The current mnav can be thought of as the expected lifetime BTC yield of MSTR.

### Arguments for below 1 mnav
- Tax Liability — MSTR's cost basis for its bitcoin is around 75k. If it ever sold bitcoin it would owe taxes. Liquidation value is less than the value of the bitcoin it currently holds. Ironically, if bitcoin were to fall below the cost basis, it could actually be a tax benefit.

### Company Specific
What went wrong with the trade:
- Mnav expanded
- Bitcoin rocketed up
- Sized the position too large at 5%
- MSTR has been able to accretively dilute to purchase more Bitcoin — didn't assume any of this in initial modeling. Assumed bitcoin would run out of steam and mnav would return to 1 before Saylor could really tap the ATM.

On such a highly speculative position, should have limited to 1% initial size because of uncapped losses. Or 5% as long as there are hedges to prevent losing more than 5%.
