## Overview

## Thesis

### Shareholder Friendly?
Management incentive structure is questionable. Is this really in the best interest of shareholders?

https://ir.navios-mlp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/navios-maritime-partners-lp-reports-financial-results-second-15

The 10 year contract ending in 2035 is as follows:
 1. a technical management fee of $950 per day per owned vessel
 2. a commercial management fee of 1.25% on revenues
 3. an S&P fee of 1% on purchase or sales price
 4. fees for other specialized services (e.g. supervision of newbuilding vessels). Fixed fees to be adjusted annually for United States Consumer Price Index.
 5. The Management Agreement also allows for stated incentive awards if equity returns exceed 15%, upon the unanimous consent of the Board of Directors of Navios Partners.
 6. The Agreements provide for payment of a termination fee, which equals the estimated amount of fees for the duration of the contract

Under this contract, management is incentivized to grow the fleet and earn more off fees rather than focus on shareholder returns.

This is concerning however they authorized 100m in buybacks of which they currently have used ~10m. 100m is about 6% of the company. So far since Q1 they have only repurchased ~0.6% of the shares outstanding. Hopefully the pace increases significantly but I'm not counting on it.
