## Danaos Corporation

Idea: [[Cyclicals]]

Industry: [[Ship Lessors]]

Last update: 2025-02-20

### Overview: 
Danaos Corporation owns and leases ships. Their fleet is composed mostly of [[Container Ships]], supplemented by dry [[Bulk Carriers]]. They typically lease these ships on contracts ranging from 1 to 5 years. 

### Thesis: 
Danaos is priced for bankruptcy despite its large contracted revenue, high margins, and minimal debt. It also trades at a discount to its ship lessor peers despite having a better balance sheet and higher margins. 

It currently trades at
- 3.1x [[PE]] (2024 expected EPS)
- 0.55 P/[[TBV]]

With only 0.40Net Debt/[[EBITDA]].

Recent 800mm loan though not included in above figure
### Earnings:
Because Danaos leases ships and has 3.4B in contracted revenue with an average charter term of 3.7 years, it's earnings are highly predictable into 2026. Contracted operating days charter coverage for the container vessel fleet is currently 97% for 2025 and 79% for 2026. 

### Balance Sheet:
The balance sheet is not useful as ship values are not marked to market. However, based on Q1 2024 info DAC has a Price to Tangible Book Value of 0.55. In a recent interview, the CEO was commended for his decision to purchase 8 dry bulk ships, as prices shot up after the purchase. Thus if anything, it’s likely that their ships are currently undervalued on the balance sheet.

### History:

![[DAC history.png]]

Danaos Corporation, founded in 1972 in Piraeus, Greece, transitioned from managing dry bulk vessels to focusing on container ships in the early 1990s. In 2006, Danaos went public with an IPO on the NYSE, raising funds for fleet expansion. However, this fleet expansion occurred at an inopportune time and led to significant trouble during the 2008 financial crisis. Danaos had entered into interest rate swaps to guarantee their ability to repay their debts but these rate swaps turned into significant liabilities on their balance sheet as rates went to zero during the GFC. This in turn caused them to violate debt covenants, however all throughout the crisis and in the following years Danaos was able to repay their debt on time as they had thoughtfully planned out their cash flows and debt maturities. 

From 2010 to 2015, Danaos was able to more than double their [[TBV]] from 50 to 107. 

In 2016, Danaos Corporation faced significant challenges due to the financial instability of two major clients, Hanjin Shipping and Hyundai Merchant Marine (HMM). The bankruptcy of Hanjin Shipping had a substantial impact on Danaos, as it led to the cessation of revenue recognition from eight vessels chartered to Hanjin, effective July 1, 2016. This resulted in a \$24.8 million loss in operating revenues for the third quarter of 2016 and a $15.8 million bad debt expense​ ([Danaos](https://www.danaos.com/news-and-media/press-release-details/2016/Danaos-Corporation-Reports-Third-Quarter-and-Nine-Months-Results-for-the-Period-Ended-September-30-2016/default.aspx))​​ ([Danaos](https://www.danaos.com/news-and-media/press-release-details/2017/Danaos-Corporation-Reports-Results-for-the-Fourth-Quarter-and-Year-Ended-December-31-2016/default.aspx))​.

Meanwhile, HMM also encountered financial difficulties but managed to avoid bankruptcy through a debt restructuring agreement supported by the South Korean government. Despite this, Danaos recorded a $12.9 million impairment loss on HMM securities due to the instability​ ([Danaos](https://www.danaos.com/news-and-media/press-release-details/2016/Danaos-Corporation-Reports-Third-Quarter-and-Nine-Months-Results-for-the-Period-Ended-September-30-2016/default.aspx))​. The combined impact of these events placed considerable pressure on Danaos's financials, leading to decreased fleet utilization and lower re-chartering rates.

To manage the significant debt maturities in 2016, Danaos negotiated debt forgiveness and restructuring agreements with lenders. These measures were crucial in helping Danaos navigate the financial difficulties exacerbated by the issues with its major clients​ ([Danaos](https://www.danaos.com/news-and-media/press-release-details/2017/Danaos-Corporation-Reports-Results-for-the-Fourth-Quarter-and-Year-Ended-December-31-2016/default.aspx))​.

In 2018 another client, ZIM, was facing bankruptcy and owed 200M to Danaos. As part of a restructuring deal which Danoas helped organize, Danaos lowered the leasing rates, and agreed to forgive the debt in exchange for 50M in bonds, and 150M in ZIM equity, making Danaos the second largest shareholder in ZIM. After selling the remaining ZIM shares in 2022, Danaos ended up with ~$600M. 

(Listen to the CEO, John Coustas talk about the 2008 financial crisis, 2016 challenges, and the deal with ZIM in [this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w5QqOc4S4Rc) 2022 interview). 

The latest major event for them was the 2020 Covid-19 pandemic and its impact on global shipping which at first was extremely negative, sending the stock down to a low of $3.33, but ultimately turned out to be very fortunate for Danaos. As freight rates soared due to increased demand for goods, so too did spot rates for ships. During the subsequent years Danaos has been able to pay due almost all of its debt, leaving it with no net debt as of today. 

Recently, Danaos has decided to expand into the dry bulk sector, as they believe that that sector is at a good place in the cycle.
