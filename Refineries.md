# Refineries

*Tangential to [[oil thesis]]. Last updated: 2026-07-22 (inventory + Hormuz balance added).*

Two refining stories matter for the oil/product complex right now: **China** (world's largest refining system, swing product exporter) and **Russia** (world's third-largest, major diesel exporter, under sustained Ukrainian drone campaign).

---

## China: why they're not exporting

It's mostly **policy**, not physical inability. Two separate levers:

### 1. Export restrictions (the binding constraint on global supply)

- Beijing controls clean product exports via **quotas + ad hoc permission**, not just economics.
- **March 2026:** After Iran war / Hormuz fears, China effectively shut most gasoline, diesel, and jet exports to secure domestic supply. Private exporters (e.g. ZPC/Rongsheng) halted entirely; state refiners could only export via monthly applications.
- **April–June:** Partial easing as stockpiles built, but exports stayed well below normal (~600–800 kt/month vs. ~3 Mt/month when unrestricted).
- **July 2026:** Restrictions lifted for the rest of July — refiners targeting ~3 Mt exports (roughly a normal month). August TBD; compliance requirements remain (inventories must stay above end-February levels, quota-only volumes).
- Export quotas on paper are fine (~32 Mt YTD, similar to 2025). The constraint is **Beijing's willingness to let product leave**, not quota availability.

### 2. Domestic price controls (the binding constraint on run rates)

- China uses a **state-set retail price ceiling** mechanism (2016 rules, NDRC). Gasoline and diesel ceilings adjust every 10 working days based on a basket of international crude prices — *in theory* a pass-through between $40–130/bbl Brent equivalent.
- NDRC retains discretionary power to pause, delay, or **narrow** adjustments during emergencies (Article 7 of the 2016 rules). This is the key lever — not a fixed subsidy, but the ability to cap how much of crude cost inflation reaches the pump.

**Two-phase 2026 story:**

| Phase | Timing | Policy | Effect on refiners |
|-------|--------|--------|-------------------|
| **Crisis caps** | Mar–Apr | First-ever use of discretionary intervention. Capped hikes at ~half formula — e.g. +420 yuan/t gas vs. +800 formula-driven | Massive squeeze. Teapots ~-$6/bbl, state-owned ~-$18/bbl. Runs collapsed to ~50% in Shandong |
| **Partial relief** | Jun | Allowed run cuts to 80% of year-ago levels (down from "maintain two-year average" mandate) | Permission to cut, not incentive to ramp |
| **Reopening package** | Early–mid Jul | See below | Margins improving; teapots encouraged back |

**July policy shift (past ~2 weeks) — teapot reopening + price pass-through:**

Beijing appears to be pivoting from "shield consumers at all costs" toward "let refiners earn enough to run":

1. **Shandong teapot support (early July):** Provincial government raised consumption tax rebates for 6 independent refiners (Chambroad, Hongrun, Lihuayi, Xinyue, Jincheng, Xintai) by 25 percentage points to **75–95%** on gasoline/diesel refined from imported fuel oil. Explicit goal: improve teapot profitability and get plants running again.
2. **Export curbs lifted (early July):** Clean product exports permitted for rest of July (~3 Mt target).
3. **Retail price hike (Jul 17, effective Jul 18):** Gasoline **+300 yuan/t**, diesel **+290 yuan/t** — appears to be **full formula pass-through** (no "调控后实际上调" capped language, unlike March/April). NDRC simultaneously directed all refiners to maintain production and ensure supply.
4. **Price cycle context:** NDRC had actually *cut* prices on Jul 3 before this hike — so the Jul 17 move is the first meaningful upward pass-through in weeks.

Early March teapots were running to cash in on a domestic price surge (+20–30% at the pump since Iran war). Then caps killed that trade. The July package looks like Beijing trying to restore it — higher retail prices + tax rebates + export permission.

**Bottom line:** Export restriction was the main global supply lever. Price controls were the main domestic run-rate lever. Both are now easing, but gradually and conditionally — not a full liberalization.

### Utilization and spare capacity

| Period | CDU utilization | Throughput | Notes |
|--------|-----------------|------------|-------|
| 2024 | ~75.5% | ~14.2 mmbpd | Already below US levels (~90%+) |
| 2025 | ~77% | ~14.75 mmbpd | Record throughput, still below nameplate |
| Jun 2026 | **~58%** | **~12.5 mmbpd** | Sharpest y/y drop in 20+ years; near 10-year low |

- **Nameplate capacity:** ~19.3 mmbpd (~963 Mt/year)
- **Spare vs. today (58%):** ~6.8 mmbpd
- **Spare vs. 90% utilization:** would add ~5 mmbpd from current levels, ~2.5 mmbpd from 2025 average

China was already oversized for domestic demand pre-crisis (EV drag, weak gasoil). Even at 75% utilization in 2024–25 they exported ~50–58 Mt/year of products. They're the global **swing supplier** — when quotas allow and margins work, product flows out; when Beijing gets nervous, flows stop.

### Go-forward: China

**What's changed (Jul 2026):** The July package hits all three levers — teapot rebates, export reopening, retail price pass-through. This is the most constructive policy shift for global product supply since the March export ban. But it's early, conditional, and August is uncertain.

**What still needs to happen for China to materially ease global product tightness:**

1. **Sustained export reopening** — July normalization is relief, not a flood. Need August+ extension without re-tightening if Hormuz/Iran risk returns.
2. **Continued formula pass-through** — Jul 17 hike is encouraging; need more cycles without re-imposing caps. Analysts flag risk of another hike at Jul 31 window if crude stays elevated.
3. **Teapot run recovery** — Shandong was at ~50% in June. Tax rebates + better retail margins should help, but teapots need crude import quotas and affordable feedstock too. Ramp to 60–65% plausible; back to 75%+ less so near-term.
4. **Crude supply** — May imports fell to ~7.8 mmbpd vs. ~11.6 mmbpd avg. Drawing stocks to avoid expensive crude. Can't sustain 90% runs without more imports or continued inventory draw.
5. **Domestic demand** — structurally weak; inventories building. Incremental throughput would mostly need to exit via exports.

**Base case (updated):** Partial normalization underway — exports ~2.5–3 Mt/month in Jul, teapot runs recovering modestly, retail prices tracking formula again. Runs drift from ~58% toward low-70s over Q3 if crude stabilizes. Modest crack relief in Asia, not a collapse.

**Bull case for product tightness:** Export curbs re-tighten (Iran escalation), NDRC re-imposes price caps, teapots stay sub-55%. China stays on sidelines.

**Bear case for cracks:** July package extends through Q3 — sustained exports + formula pass-through + teapot ramp toward 70–75% utilization. Could add ~0.5–1.0 mmbpd of product to global market over current depressed levels. Material for Asia; still may not fully offset Russia's lost product exports.

## Russia: refining degradation

### Current state (Jul 2026)

- **Nameplate capacity:** ~6.3–6.6 mmbpd (34 refineries)
- **Current runs:** ~3.8 mmbpd — lowest in 21+ years
- **Effectively offline:** ~1.5–2.0 mmbpd processing capacity at any given time
- **Capacity affected by attacks:** Kpler estimates ~4.3 mmbpd (~58% of total) has faced downtime in July; OIES notes 24 of 34 refineries attacked in 2026, covering 5.1 mmbpd (81% of capacity) at least once
- Russia has **banned diesel exports** domestically to manage its own fuel crisis — tightening global supply further
- Secondary units (FCC, hydrocrackers, reformers) take **months** to repair; refineries are **re-struck before repairs complete** (Ryazan hit 15+ times)

### Why degradation accelerates

- Ukrainian drones reaching deeper targets (Omsk 2,500 km from border, Taneco 1,200 km)
- Targeting shift from simple CDUs to complex conversion units → longer, more expensive outages
- Emergency repairs degrade structural integrity; repeated heat/cool cycles stress equipment
- Drone production scaling (Firepoint ~100 FP-1s/day, ~10% hit rate, expanding)
- Russia's air defense appears unable to protect dispersed refining assets at this scale/intensity
- Kpler expects only modest recovery to ~4.3 mmbpd in August, ~4.6 in September — still well below historical levels. No meaningful recovery before Q4 unless attacks ease.

### Forward view: 75% degraded?

**Reasonable framing of "75% degraded":**

| Interpretation | Current | Plausible in months? |
|----------------|---------|----------------------|
| 75% of capacity **attacked/damaged** at least once | ~81% already (OIES) | Already there |
| 75% of capacity **offline simultaneously** | ~25–40% offline at peak | Aggressive but not impossible if intensity holds |
| Runs fall to ~25% of nameplate (~1.5 mmbpd) | ~60% of nameplate today | Would require sustained campaign + failed repairs |

**Thesis:** At current pace, Russia is unlikely to stabilize refining. The system is being degraded faster than it can be repaired. Ukraine has every incentive to keep hitting infrastructure (domestic Russian pressure, export revenue loss, war visibility). Russia has limited options — can't easily replace complex Western refinery equipment, can't defend 34 scattered sites, and export bans are a sign of desperation not control.

**Implications:**

- **Product markets:** Structural diesel/gasoil tightness. European and Asian cracks at/near 10-year highs (some surpassing 2022). NW Europe diesel margins >$60/bbl.
- **Crude markets:** Less refining → more crude available for export (Russia already below OPEC+ quota). Bullish products, mixed/ambiguous crude depending on how much displaced Russian crude clears.
- **China offset:** Even if China fully normalized exports (~600–800 kbpd product), Russia's lost product exports are in the same ballpark (~1.0–1.5 mmbpd). And China isn't fully normalizing.
- **Net:** Product tightness is a multi-quarter structural theme unless Ukraine attacks stop or China materially changes both export and pricing policy.

## Scenario: China 90% / Russia 25% / Gulf products offline

*Stress-test for product market balance. Rough order-of-magnitude — yields and export shares are assumptions.*

### Baseline anchors

| Actor | Nameplate | "Normal" runs | Current (~Jul 2026) | Stress scenario |
|-------|-----------|---------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **China** | ~19.3 mmbpd | ~14.8 mmbpd (~77%) | ~12.5 mmbpd (~58%) | **17.4 mmbpd (90%)** |
| **Russia** | ~6.5 mmbpd | ~5.5 mmbpd (~85%) | ~3.8 mmbpd (~58%) | **1.6 mmbpd (25%)** |
| **Gulf product exports** | — | ~6 mmbpd via Hormuz* | ~0.5–1.0 mmbpd | ~0.5–1.0 (unchanged) |

\*Pre-war Hormuz petroleum total ~21 mmbpd: ~15 crude + **~6 refined products** (JKemp/IEA). The "~3 mmbpd" figure often cited is a narrower slice (clean products from specific Gulf exporters, or gasoline/diesel/jet only). Either way, the product side has recovered far slower than crude.

**Gulf product recovery asymmetry (key point):**
- Crude through Hormuz briefly recovered during late Jun ceasefire (~12 mmbpd exits Jul 1–11); **collapsed again to ~1 mmbpd by Jul 20** as truce ended Jul 8
- **Refined products: only ~1 mmbpd exporting in June — ~25% of pre-conflict levels** (Kpler). IEA: Gulf product + LPG exports still below half of pre-war.
- Net Hormuz shortfall when shut: **~12 mmbpd** (20 pre-war − ~7–8 bypass). See [[#Global balance, inventories & Hormuz]].

### Product math (using ~82% yield from crude throughput)

**Important distinction:** Incremental *production* ≠ incremental *exports*. China going 58% → 90% adds ~**4.0 mmbpd of gross product output** to the global system. How much of that reaches international markets depends on domestic absorption and export policy.

**China at 90% vs. today (58%):**

| | Throughput | Product output (~82% yield) |
|--|------------|-------------------------------|
| Today (58%) | 12.5 mmbpd | ~10.3 mmbpd |
| Scenario (90%) | 17.4 mmbpd | ~14.3 mmbpd |
| **Increment** | **+4.9 mmbpd** | **+4.0 mmbpd** |

That +4.0 mmbpd is real — it's more gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel being produced. The question is where it goes:

1. **Domestic consumption** — China still uses ~13–14 mmbpd of products. At 58% runs they're *undershooting* domestic needs (~10.3 produced vs. ~13+ consumed), drawing inventories. The first ~2.5–3.0 mmbpd of the increment refills that domestic gap.
2. **Exports** — what's left after domestic needs. At 90% with export permission, maybe **+1.0–1.5 mmbpd** of *additional exportable* volume vs. today (~0.5–0.9 mmbpd currently). Could be higher if they're willing to dump product.

So: **+4.0 mmbpd production, but maybe only +1.0–1.5 mmbpd hits the global export market.** Both numbers are correct; they measure different things.

**Russia at 25% vs. today (3.8 mmbpd runs):**

| | Throughput | Product output |
|--|------------|----------------|
| Today | 3.8 mmbpd | ~3.1 mmbpd |
| Scenario (25%) | 1.6 mmbpd | ~1.3 mmbpd |
| **Increment** | **-2.2 mmbpd** | **-1.8 mmbpd** |

Russia loses **-1.8 mmbpd of product production** vs. today. Exports are already near zero, so the global export market barely feels the marginal change — but total global product supply drops.

**China vs. Russia — two ways to count it:**

| Comparison | Product production change | Export market change |
|------------|--------------------------|---------------------|
| China 90% vs. today | **+4.0 mmbpd** | **+1.0–1.5 mmbpd** (after domestic fill) |
| Russia 25% vs. today | **-1.8 mmbpd** | **-0.2 to -0.5 mmbpd** (exports already gutted) |
| **Net vs. today** | **+2.2 mmbpd** production | **+0.5–1.3 mmbpd** exports |

vs. pre-crisis normal:

| Comparison | Product production change | Export market change |
|------------|--------------------------|---------------------|
| China 90% vs. normal (77%) | **+2.2 mmbpd** | **+1.0–2.0 mmbpd** |
| Russia 25% vs. normal (85%) | **-3.2 mmbpd** | **-2.4 mmbpd** (lost exports) |
| **Net vs. normal** | **-1.0 mmbpd** production | **~-0.4 to -1.4 mmbpd** exports |

**China vs. Russia direct offset (corrected):**
- On **total production**: China +4.0 vs. Russia -1.8 (vs. today) → net **+2.2 mmbpd** more products in the world. China wins clearly.
- On **exports/tradeable supply**: China +1.0–1.5 vs. Russia -0.2 to -0.5 (vs. today) → net **+0.8–1.3 mmbpd**. Modest improvement.
- vs. normal: China doesn't fully replace Russia's lost **-2.4 mmbpd** of exports, and neither touches the Gulf.

### Stacked deficit vs. pre-crisis normal

| Source of product supply | vs. normal | Approx. deficit |
|--------------------------|------------|-----------------|
| Gulf product exports (6 → 1 mmbpd) | Hormuz/refinery disruption | **-5.0 mmbpd** |
| Russia product exports (2.4 → 0) | Drone strikes + export bans | **-2.4 mmbpd** |
| China product exports (1.0 → 0.5) | Export curbs + run cuts | **-0.5 mmbpd** |
| Global refinery runs (IEA) | Broad underutilization | **~-6.0 mmbpd** throughput vs. y/y |
| **Total product market hole** | | **~-7 to -8 mmbpd** |

**If China runs at 90% AND Russia falls to 25% (vs. today):**

| Adjustment | Production | Exports |
|------------|------------|---------|
| China 58% → 90% | **+4.0 mmbpd** | **+1.0–1.5 mmbpd** |
| Russia 3.8 → 1.6 mmbpd | **-1.8 mmbpd** | **-0.2 to -0.5 mmbpd** |
| **Net China–Russia** | **+2.2 mmbpd** | **+0.8–1.3 mmbpd** |
| Gulf still down vs. normal | — | **-5.0 mmbpd** |
| **Net vs. pre-crisis normal** | still short | **~-3.7 to -4.2 mmbpd** export deficit |

China at 90% **does** add ~4 mmbpd of product production vs. today — that's real and meaningful. But ~2.5–3 mmbpd of it fills China's own domestic gap (they're currently undersupplying themselves at 58% runs). Only ~1–1.5 mmbpd becomes new exportable supply. Meanwhile the Gulf hole is **-5 mmbpd** on its own.

### vs. today (Jul 2026)

| Change | Production impact | Export market impact |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------|
| China 58% → 90% | **+4.0 mmbpd** | **+1.0–1.5 mmbpd** |
| Russia 3.8 → 1.6 mmbpd runs | **-1.8 mmbpd** | **-0.2 to -0.5 mmbpd** |
| Gulf products stay at ~1 mmbpd | no change | still **-5.0 vs. normal** |

Net vs. today: **+2.2 mmbpd** more product production globally from China/Russia combined. Modest export relief (+0.8–1.3). Russia also pushes **+1.5–2.0 mmbpd crude** to export market instead of refining it (bearish crude, doesn't help products).

### Crude vs. products — why this matters for the thesis

The market is splitting:
- **Crude:** Gulf crude partially recovered, Russia exporting more unrefined barrels, China drawing inventories → crude looks better supplied
- **Products:** Gulf exports collapsed, Russia can't refine, China not exporting enough → cracks blow out even as crude eases

This is exactly the "refining is the binding constraint" thesis. You can have crude and still not have diesel. Emergency stock releases and inventory draws bridge crude gaps; they don't create refining capacity.

**Bottom line for the scenario:** China at 90% adds **~4 mmbpd of product production** vs. today — that's real. But most of it (~2.5–3 mmbpd) fills a domestic gap because they're currently undersupplying themselves at 58% runs. Only **~1–1.5 mmbpd** becomes new exportable supply. Russia at 25% removes **~1.8 mmbpd** of production vs. today. Net production: **+2.2 mmbpd** — China clearly wins on volume. But the Gulf product deficit (**~-5 mmbpd** vs. normal) dwarfs all of it. Until Gulf refineries restart and Hormuz product flows normalize (Kpler: 3–4 months), product tightness persists.

## Global balance, inventories & Hormuz

### Hormuz net shortfall (do not double-count bypass)

Pre-war Hormuz petroleum flows: **~20 mmbpd** total (crude + products + condensate/LPG).

When the strait is effectively shut, **~7–8 mmbpd** still moves via bypass routes (Saudi East-West pipeline → Yanbu, UAE Habshan–Fujairah, Iraq via Turkey, etc.). That bypass is **already netted in** the headline shortfall:

```
~20 mmbpd pre-war Hormuz flows
− ~7–8 mmbpd bypass (still operating)
─────────────────────────────────
= ~12 mmbpd net supply shortfall when shut
```

**Do not subtract bypass again on top of 12.** The 12 is net.

### Hormuz status (Jul 22, 2026)

Effectively **shut again** after US–Iran truce collapsed **Jul 8**:

| Period | Hormuz crude exits (S&P Global) |
|--------|--------------------------------|
| Jul 1–11 | ~12 mmbpd |
| Since Jul 12 | ~3 mmbpd avg |
| Last 4 days (as of Jul 20) | ~1 mmbpd |
| Jul 22 | **Zero VLCC/LNG tankers**; 3 commercial ships total (Reuters/CBS) |

- Iran: no departures authorized while hostilities continue; IRGC warns southern route **mined**
- US: 11th night of strikes; insists strait "open" — shippers not transiting
- 7+ tanker attacks since Jul 11
- Gulf **product** exports still ~0.5–1.0 mmbpd vs ~6 pre-war — products not recovering even when crude briefly did

Brief ceasefire reopening (late Jun–early Jul) pushed a glut of oil onto the water (~117 mb build in Jun per IEA). That masked draws and kept Brent near **$70**. With flows back to ~1 mmbpd, that cushion is burning off fast.

### Global balance identity

Only three things close a total petroleum balance:

```
Net production shortfall = Total inventory draw + True end-use demand destruction
```

**Not balancing items** (trade flows or transformations, already in inventory data):
- Import cuts / China import collapse (barrels relocate, don't vanish)
- Atlantic Basin → Asia (trade reallocation)
- Refinery run cuts (shifts crude ↔ product stocks; total oil draw is what matters)

**IEA observed inventory draw since war start:** **3.8 mmbpd** average (crude **2.4** + products **1.4**). Includes commercial + government stocks (IEA 400M release is inside this, not on top). May peak: **4.6 mmbpd**. June: onshore drew 96 mb but oil-on-water **+117 mb** → net observed **+21 mb** that month.

At a sustained **12 mmbpd net** shortfall, 3.8 avg understates current pressure — that average smooths over the reopening glut.

At April peak (~$120 Brent), JPM still saw **~2 mmbpd residual gap** after 7.1 mmbpd inventory draw. Real price-driven demand destruction at $120 was probably **1–2 mmbpd**, not 4–5.

**Implication:** At **~$70** with Hormuz shut and ~12 net shortfall, the market has not begun the demand destruction needed to balance. April showed $120 wasn't enough to fully clear the market.

### Data blind spots

**China:** SPR/commercial stocks are state secrets. Last official disclosure 2017/2019. Kpler flagged **~140 mb "unaccounted"** pre-war — baseline may be understated, making observed draws look smaller. Material balance (imports + production − runs) implies **~1–2 mmbpd draw**; only ~1 mmbpd shows cleanly in IEA observed data.

**Products:** Satellite monitoring (Kayrros, Kpler) tracks **floating-roof crude tanks**. Product storage is mostly fixed-roof, spheres, underground — **Kayrros tracks crude only, no products**. The 1.4 mmbpd product draw in IEA's 3.8 is the least reliable component. Product tightness (record cracks) may be running off **invisible product inventory draws**.

Much of what gets labeled "demand destruction" is probably **oil leaving tanks we can't count**.

### Crude vs products — why the market looks schizophrenic

| | Crude | Products |
|--|-------|----------|
| Supply | Partially recovered during brief reopening; 18M bbl floating with no buyers; discounts $6–9/bbl | Gulf exports dead; Russia gutted; structural hole |
| Demand | China drawing stocks, not importing (−4–5 mmbpd import swing) | Summer travel; less elastic |
| Price | Brent **~$70–77** | Cracks at **records** |
| Signal | Loose / no panic | Tight / panicking |

**Thesis:** Cracks are the price signal. Refiners will eventually run harder (margin incentive). That compresses cracks but **pulls crude demand** — China 58% → 80% alone is **+3+ mmbpd** crude imports. Crude is the beneficiary on the ramp.

Asia ex-China not at capacity limits — running below pre-war on **price discipline and uncertainty**, not physical inability. US refiners already in max-output mode (~93%).

### When does crude panic buying start?

Not at "X months of global stocks left." Triggers are **regional buffer exhaustion + behavioral flip**:

| Milestone | Timing (rough) |
|-----------|----------------|
| Product cracks at records | **Now** |
| Hormuz shut, flows ~1 mmbpd | **Now** (Jul 22) |
| IEA release fully deployed | ~1–2 months |
| US SPR at statutory floor | ~2 months at current pace |
| Japan below 180 days cover | ~2–3 months |
| China flips drawer → buyer (+6 mmbpd swing) | Goldman flagged Jul–Aug; policy-driven |
| Inventory draws re-accelerate post-glut | Weeks if Hormuz stays shut |
| Brent back to $120+ | Could be weeks, not months, if flows stay near zero |

April already panicked at $120 without anyone running out of oil. Current $70 with Hormuz at zero tanker transits looks mispriced unless the market assumes another temporary reopening.

**Panic catalyst for crude:** China restock headline + refiners committing to crude for crack capture + Hormuz stays shut while draws accelerate. Products are already panicking; crude hasn't caught up because the biggest buyer is sitting out.

## How the two interact

```
Russia loses refining → global product supply down → cracks blow out
China restricts exports + caps prices + cuts runs (Mar–Jun) → removes swing supply
China July package: teapot rebates + export reopening + retail hike → modest relief starting
Russia continues degrading → ongoing product deficit; may overwhelm China offset
```

The crack spread explosion makes sense as a **stacked** shock: Russia downstream crisis + Middle East disruption + low global inventories + China double pullback (runs + exports). The July China pivot is real but may be too small and too late vs. accelerating Russian degradation.

**Key variables to watch:**

- [ ] China August export policy
- [ ] NDRC Jul 31 pricing window — continued pass-through or re-imposition of caps?
- [ ] Shandong teapot run rates (was ~50% in Jun; recovering?)
- [ ] China refinery run rates nationally (was ~58% in Jun)
- [ ] Russia refinery runs and re-attack frequency
- [ ] Russia diesel export ban duration
- [ ] Global product inventories (ARA, Singapore, US PADDs) — **poorly measured vs crude**
- [ ] Hormuz tanker transits / crude exit flows (was ~1 mmbpd as of Jul 20)
- [ ] China import data (flip from drawer to buyer = crude panic catalyst)
- [ ] OECD commercial + government stock levels (lowest since 1990)
- [ ] IEA emergency release remaining (~124M of 400M)
